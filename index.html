Stencil pieces

Striker (3D print)

Bottom design

The 3MF is one object with 3 parts: body, colour 2 and colour 3. Open it in Bambu Studio and give each part a filament. Print the design face down on the bed, 100% infill. Sand the bottom lightly (0.3 mm at most) and take the thickness off the top. Use the plain STL to check the weight in the slicer.

Coins (3D print)

Top face

Change the diameter under Measurements, Coin Ø. The bottom face stays flat and plain, because it slides on the board. Sand and polish the bottom, then the top. Polish lightly so the grooves stay; 1 mm deep grooves leave room for that. Official coins weigh 5.0 to 5.5 g, so the file sets the infill for you. Check the weight in Bambu Studio before you print.

Pocket rims and feet (3D print)

Foot design
Foot height

Print flat face down, no supports. PETG or PLA, 4 walls, 30% infill. Screws: 4 per rim and 3 per foot. Pick the type and length under Screws in the settings. Drill 2.5 mm pilot holes, 9 mm deep. The rim hole is 1 mm bigger than the pocket, so no edge catches a coin. Tie a cloth bag in the groove on the collar. Put each separate foot about 110 mm in from both edges, so it clears the rim. Joined feet need no screws of their own: the rim holds them.

Rails (frame, fences)

Set the rail sizes on the Design tab, under "Rails (fences)".

Rail marks (optional)
  • Quarters split each rail into 4 equal parts. Use them as aiming points for bank shots: the striker leaves a rail at the same angle it hits it, so a fixed mark helps you pick the spot. Pool tables use the same idea. Pick these if you like rebound shots.
  • Baselines line up with the red circles at the ends of the baselines. From anywhere at the table you can see where the shooting zone starts and ends, and line up straight shots along a baseline. Pick these for beginners, or to judge striker placement.
  • Both gives 5 marks per rail, with the centre. Pick one set if you want clean rails.

Hardwood works best: birch, beech, maple or oak. The rails must be straight and square, because the pieces bounce off them. Glue and screw them to the panel edge, and keep the top edge level all the way round. Keep rail marks small: 3 mm dots or thin lines on the top face. Measure them from the same corner on every rail.

Finish the surface

  1. Seal the wood. Sand the bare wood smooth and wipe off the dust. Spray 1 or 2 light coats of clear. Matte is fine here. This stops the paint bleeding into the grain, so the lines stay sharp.
  2. Paint. Spray the stencils, as the README says.
  3. Protect the paint. Spray 1 more light coat of clear.
  4. Wait 3 to 7 days in a warm place, 15 °C or more. Urethane on soft spray paint can wrinkle or stay sticky.
  5. Scuff. Use 320 to 400 grit until the surface looks dull. Go very gently over the painted lines. Wipe off the dust.
  6. Top coat. Put on 2 or 3 thin coats of water-based polyurethane, satin or gloss, with a foam sponge. Do not go back over wet areas, so no bubbles form. Sand lightly with 400 grit between coats.
  7. Let it harden for a few days. Then dust on carrom powder and play.

Only the top coat changes how the striker slides: satin or gloss slides best. Spray cans dry slowly below 10 °C. Test the full finish on a scrap piece first.

Pocket bags

  1. Use old socks. They stretch, they are soft, so the coins drop quietly, and they cost nothing. Thick, dark sports socks work best.
  2. Wash them, then cut off the toe end. The toe is the closed bottom of the bag.
  3. Stretch the open end over the collar under each pocket. The collar is about 52 mm across.
  4. Tie it in the groove on the collar with a rubber band, a zip tie or string.

The goal

Flick the striker to knock your coins into the pockets. The first player to pocket all of their coins wins the board. One of you plays white, the other plays black. The red queen belongs to whoever pockets it and then covers it.

What you need

  • The board, dusted with a little carrom powder so the pieces glide.
  • 9 white coins, 9 black coins and 1 red queen.
  • 1 striker, bigger and heavier than the coins.

No carrom powder? Make your own

  • Cornflour (cornstarch): the easiest swap. Sift it through a tea strainer.
  • Talc-free baby powder: it is mostly cornstarch, and it smells nice.
  • Potato starch: very fine and slippery.
  • Our mix: 2 spoons cornflour + 1 spoon potato starch. Sift it twice and keep it in a salt shaker.

Use only a pinch and spread it with your palm. Do not use wheat flour or sugar: they get sticky. Some players use boric acid powder. It is harmful if swallowed, so do not use it around children or pets. Wipe the board before you put it away.

Setting up

  1. Put the queen on the centre spot.
  2. Put 6 coins in a ring around the queen, all touching her.
  3. Put the other 12 coins in a ring around those, all touching.
  4. Toss a coin. The winner breaks and plays white.

Taking a shot

  • Shoot from your own side. Stay seated and do not move your chair.
  • Put the striker between your two baselines. It must touch both lines, or cover the red circle at the end completely.
  • Flick the striker with one finger. Do not push it.
  • The striker must not touch the arrow line.
  • Shoot forwards: the striker must cross your front baseline. To hit a coin behind your baseline, bounce the striker off a rail.
  • The break: the first shot of a board. You get 3 tries to move the coins in the centre.

Your turn

  • Pocket one of your coins: you shoot again. Keep going until you miss.
  • Pocket nothing: your turn ends.
  • Pocket only your opponent's coin: it stays in the pocket and counts for them. Your turn ends.
  • Pocket your coin and theirs together: both stay in, and you shoot again.
  • A coin that ends up standing on its edge or on another coin stays where it is.

The queen

You may pocket the queen only after you have pocketed at least one of your own coins. Then you must cover her: pocket one of your coins with the next shot, or in the same shot.

  • Covered: the queen is yours.
  • Not covered: she goes back on the centre spot, and your turn ends.
  • You may not pocket your last coin while the queen is still on the board. That is a foul.

Fouls

After a foul, your turn ends. You pay a penalty: one of your pocketed coins goes back onto the board. Your opponent places it inside the centre circle. If you have no pocketed coin yet, you owe one and pay it as soon as you pocket a coin.

These are fouls:

  • The striker goes into a pocket. If one of your coins went in on the same shot, it comes back as well.
  • The striker or a coin jumps off the board.
  • You touch a coin with your hand, or you shoot from a wrong place.
  • You pocket your last coin before the queen is covered.

Scoring

The player who clears all their coins wins the board.

  • The winner scores 1 point for each opponent coin still on the board.
  • The winner adds 3 points for the queen, if the winner covered her. These 3 points stop counting once the winner has 22 points or more.
  • The loser scores nothing for that board.
  • A game ends when one player reaches 25 points, or after 8 boards. Most points wins.

Four players

Partners sit opposite each other and play the same colour. Turns pass to the right, around the table. Everything else stays the same.
